
DOCUMENT: Sample General Insurance FAQ
DOCUMENT TYPE: Insurance FAQ
DOCUMENT ID: FAQ-001
NOTICE: Fictional learning material for an insurance RAG demonstration.

1. What Is Insurance?
Insurance is a contract under which an insurer provides specified financial protection against covered risks in exchange for a premium, subject to the contract terms.

2. What Is a Premium?
A premium is the amount paid to maintain insurance coverage according to the policy's payment terms.

3. What Is a Deductible?
A deductible is the portion of an eligible expense or loss that the policyholder must bear before the insurer pays the applicable amount, according to the policy terms.

4. What Is a Policy Limit?
A policy limit is the maximum amount the insurer will pay for a covered loss or benefit under the applicable policy terms.

5. What Is a Beneficiary?
A beneficiary is a person or entity designated to receive a policy benefit when the applicable conditions for payment are satisfied.

6. What Is a Waiting Period?
A waiting period is a specified period during which certain benefits are unavailable or restricted under the policy terms.

7. What Is an Exclusion?
An exclusion is a condition, event, expense, or circumstance that the insurance policy does not cover.

8. What Is a Claim?
A claim is a request for a benefit or payment under an insurance policy.

9. Why Can a Claim Be Rejected?
A claim may be rejected when the event is excluded, policy conditions are not met, or the submitted evidence does not establish entitlement to the requested benefit. The actual reason depends on the policy and claim assessment.

10. Where Can I Find My Exact Coverage?
The policy schedule and policy wording contain the applicable coverage, limits, exclusions, and conditions. General explanations cannot replace the terms of a specific policy.

11. Can a claim be filed online?
A: Many insurers offer web portals and mobile applications for claim submission, although available options vary by insurer.

12. What should be done if documents are unavailable?
A: Claimants should inform the insurer and provide alternative documentation where acceptable.

13. Can additional documents be submitted after filing?
A: Insurers may accept supplementary information during assessment, subject to applicable procedures.

14. How is a claim reference number used?
A: The reference number helps identify, track, and manage claim activities throughout the claim lifecycle.

15. How long should claim records be retained?
A: Retention periods vary depending on regulatory, legal, financial, and organizational requirements.

16. What Is Underwriting?
A: Underwriting is the process through which an insurer evaluates risk and determines whether to accept an application, what coverage to offer, and what premium may apply.

17. What Is a Policy Schedule?
A: A policy schedule is a document that summarizes important policy information such as the policy number, insured person or property, coverage amounts, premiums, deductibles, and policy period.

18. What Is a Sum Assured?
A: A sum assured is the amount of coverage provided under a life insurance policy, subject to the policy terms and claim approval requirements.

19. What Is a Grace Period?
A: A grace period is an additional period after the premium due date during which the policyholder may make payment without immediate termination of coverage, subject to policy conditions.

20. What Happens If a Policy Lapses?
A: A policy may lapse when required premiums are not paid within the applicable period. Coverage may be reduced, suspended, or terminated according to policy terms.

21. Can a Lapsed Policy Be Reinstated?
A: Some insurers allow policy reinstatement or revival, subject to payment of overdue premiums, underwriting requirements, and other applicable conditions.

22. What Is a Nominee?
A: A nominee is a person designated by the policyholder to receive policy proceeds or benefits according to the policy provisions and applicable laws.

23. What Is a Cashless Claim?
A: A cashless claim allows eligible expenses to be settled directly between the insurer and an approved service provider such as a hospital or repair facility, subject to policy terms.

24. What Is a Reimbursement Claim?
A: Under a reimbursement claim, the policyholder pays eligible expenses first and later submits documents to the insurer for claim assessment and reimbursement.

25. What Is Pre-Authorization?
A: Pre-authorization is a process through which an insurer reviews and approves specified services, treatments, procedures, or expenses before they are incurred.

26. What Is a Network Provider?
A: A network provider is a hospital, healthcare professional, repair facility, or service provider that has an arrangement with the insurer to provide covered services.

27. What Is a Pre-Existing Condition?
A: A pre-existing condition is a medical condition, illness, injury, or symptom that existed before the policy became effective.

28. What Is Coinsurance?
A: Coinsurance is the percentage of covered expenses that may be shared between the policyholder and insurer after any applicable deductible has been satisfied.

29. What Is a Copayment?
A: A copayment is a fixed amount that a policyholder may be required to pay when receiving specified covered services.

30. What Is an Out-of-Pocket Maximum?
A: An out-of-pocket maximum is the maximum amount a policyholder may be required to pay for eligible covered expenses during a policy period.

31. What Is Third-Party Liability Coverage?
A: Third-party liability coverage may protect the insured against legal liability for injury to another person or damage to another person's property, subject to policy conditions.

32. What Is Own-Damage Coverage?
A: Own-damage coverage may protect the insured vehicle against covered physical damage resulting from specified events described in the policy.

33. What Is a No-Claim Bonus?
A: A no-claim bonus is a benefit that may be awarded to eligible policyholders who do not make claims during specified policy periods.

34. What Is a Total Loss?
A: A total loss occurs when a damaged asset cannot be economically repaired or when repair costs exceed thresholds established under the policy or insurer guidelines.

35. What Is Depreciation?
A: Depreciation refers to the reduction in value of an asset over time due to age, usage, wear, tear, or obsolescence.

36. What Is an Endorsement?
A: An endorsement is an amendment, addition, deletion, or modification to a policy that changes the original terms and conditions.

37. What Is a Rider?
A: A rider is an optional benefit attached to an insurance policy that provides additional coverage beyond the standard policy provisions.

38. Can an Insurance Policy Be Cancelled?
A: Policies may be cancelled according to policy terms, applicable laws, insurer requirements, or policyholder requests.

39. Can Coverage Be Changed After Policy Issuance?
A: Coverage modifications may be possible through endorsements, plan changes, additional riders, or other approved policy servicing processes.

40. What Is Cyber Insurance?
A: Cyber insurance is a form of insurance designed to provide protection against specified cyber-related risks and losses, subject to policy terms and exclusions.

41. What Is a Data Breach?
A: A data breach occurs when sensitive, confidential, personal, or proprietary information is accessed, disclosed, acquired, or exposed without proper authorization.

42. What Is Ransomware?
A: Ransomware is a form of malicious software that restricts access to systems or data and may demand payment in exchange for restoring access.

43. What Is Business Interruption Coverage?
A: Business interruption coverage may provide protection for qualifying financial losses resulting from specified interruptions to normal business operations.

44. What Is Cyber Extortion Coverage?
A: Cyber extortion coverage may provide protection for certain expenses associated with responding to covered cyber extortion incidents, subject to policy conditions.

45. What Documents Are Typically Required for an Insurance Claim?
A: Required documents vary by claim type but may include claim forms, identification records, invoices, receipts, reports, certificates, photographs, and other supporting evidence.

46. How Long Does Claim Processing Take?
A: Claim processing times vary depending on claim complexity, required documentation, regulatory requirements, investigations, and policy provisions.

47. Why Might Additional Information Be Requested?
A: Additional information may be requested when submitted documents are incomplete, inconsistent, unclear, or insufficient to support assessment.

48. What Is Fraud in Insurance?
A: Insurance fraud involves intentionally providing false, misleading, incomplete, or deceptive information to obtain benefits, payments, or advantages improperly.

49. What Is a Policy Renewal?
A: Policy renewal is the continuation of insurance coverage beyond the current policy period, subject to insurer approval and applicable terms.

50. Why Is Record Retention Important?
A: Maintaining policy documents, claim records, receipts, correspondence, and supporting evidence may assist with future claims, audits, disputes, compliance requirements, and administrative needs.

51. What Is Risk Assessment?
A: Risk assessment is the process of identifying, analyzing, and evaluating potential risks that may affect coverage decisions, pricing, and underwriting outcomes.

52. What Is an Insurable Interest?
A: Insurable interest exists when a person or entity would experience a financial loss or other recognized impact if the insured event occurs.

53. Can More Than One Insurance Policy Cover the Same Risk?
A: In some circumstances, multiple policies may apply to the same event, subject to policy coordination provisions and applicable regulations.

54. What Is an Insurance Certificate?
A: An insurance certificate is a document that summarizes key policy information and may serve as evidence that coverage exists.

55. What Is Policy Expiry?
A: Policy expiry occurs when the policy reaches the end of its specified coverage period and is not renewed or extended.

56. What Are Policy Conditions?
A: Policy conditions are requirements, obligations, procedures, and rules that policyholders and insurers must follow under the insurance contract.

57. What Is Claim Settlement?
A: Claim settlement is the process through which an insurer evaluates an approved claim and determines the amount payable under the policy terms.

58. Can a Claim Be Appealed?
A: Depending on applicable procedures, claimants may have the option to request reconsideration, submit additional evidence, or pursue available appeal mechanisms.

59. What Is Regulatory Compliance in Insurance?
A: Regulatory compliance refers to adherence to applicable insurance laws, consumer protection requirements, privacy obligations, reporting rules, and other legal standards.

60. Why Should Policyholders Read the Full Policy Wording?
A: The full policy wording contains the detailed coverage terms, exclusions, limitations, definitions, conditions, procedures, and obligations that govern how the policy operates.
